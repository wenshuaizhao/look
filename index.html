01 / The idea

Visual understanding
starts with awareness.

Vision-language models can describe objects that are not present. We ask a more fundamental question: whether a model is aware of the visual input available to it before interpreting its meaning.

LOOK makes that question testable. We select a rectangular crop on the model’s visual-token grid, preserve its positions from the full image, and ask the model to identify the region it can see. The crop coordinates supply exact supervision without semantic annotation.

1

Sample a view

Choose a crop aligned to complete visual tokens. Pixels outside it are absent from the input.

2

Keep its position

Retain the crop’s full-image positional information rather than treating it as a new full image.

3

Learn to locate

Predict the visible region. The chosen crop provides its own bounding-box target.

03 / Evaluation

Does the model use
the evidence?

VisABench compares the same questions with real visual evidence, absent evidence, and mismatched evidence. This probes whether a model grounds its answers in the input it actually receives.

Our experiments find that existing VLMs struggle to localize their visible region. Fine-tuning on LOOK improves visual-input awareness and reduces hallucinated object presence when evidence is absent.

The paper reports position-reset controls, data-ratio experiments, and downstream evaluations. Transfer is mixed across benchmarks; awareness gains should be read alongside real-image performance and output-format reliability.
